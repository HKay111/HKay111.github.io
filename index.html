Undergraduate Thesis: Carbon Emissions in Africa: EKC and Regional Integration

The thesis uses a panel of nine African countries — Algeria, Kenya, Mauritius, Morocco, Mozambique, Namibia, Nigeria, South Africa, and Zambia — from 2006 to 2022 to answer two interconnected questions.

Paper 1: Does regional integration affect carbon emissions in Africa? Integration is measured through regional value chains, environmental co-operation, and institutional ties. The paper uses fully modified OLS to account for cross-sectional dependence (CSD), non-stationarity, and endogeneity. The results indicate that the variables are cointegrated and move together over the long run. Trade openness and regional value chains are positively associated with consumption-based emissions. One interpretation is that, in lower-income economies, imported goods increase the emissions embodied in consumption. Environmental co-operation and institutional integration are negatively associated with emissions, suggesting that stronger regional agreements may support cleaner development. Government effectiveness is also negatively associated with emissions. GDP and per-capita energy use are positively associated with emissions, indicating that development remains emission-intensive.

Paper 2: The second paper tests for an Environmental Kuznets Curve (EKC) across African countries with different environmental profiles. It uses a group-mean fully modified OLS estimator to account for cross-sectional dependence, non-stationarity, and endogeneity. The results indicate a long-run cointegrating relationship with heterogeneous slopes. Instead of the conventional inverted U, emissions follow a U-shaped pattern: carbon emissions per capita fall as income rises, then increase beyond a threshold. The estimates also show an almost one-to-one elasticity between energy use per capita and carbon emissions per capita, pointing to a reliance on carbon-intensive energy. Without structural change in the energy mix, further growth may deepen environmental degradation, making a clean-energy transition a pressing policy priority for the region.
